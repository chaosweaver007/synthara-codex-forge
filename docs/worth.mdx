# Worth: The New Economics

## Overview

Worth represents a complete reimagining of economics for a post-scarcity world. While the Universal Diamond Standard (UDS) provides the overarching framework and standard, Worth details the specific mechanisms, protocols, and practices that implement this new economic model. It is grounded in a fundamental distinction between intrinsic worth (the inherent value of existence and potential) and realized worth (the actualized value of contribution and achievement).

The Worth model recognizes that traditional economics, based on scarcity, exchange value, and market prices, is increasingly inadequate for coordinating human activity in an age of abundance. As technology makes material goods increasingly abundant and information essentially free to reproduce, we need new ways of recognizing and rewarding contribution. Worth provides this by measuring value based on actual contribution to wellbeing, capability, and flourishing rather than market prices.

Worth is not merely a theoretical framework but a practical system with specific protocols for calculating, tracking, and exchanging value. It includes mechanisms for measuring contributions across diverse domains, aggregating individual assessments into collective valuations, and enabling exchange without requiring monetary intermediation. The system is designed to make visible and valuable the forms of labor and contribution that traditional economics often renders invisible.

## Key Concepts

### Intrinsic vs. Realized Worth

The fundamental distinction in Worth economics is between intrinsic worth (the value inherent in existence, potential, and being) and realized worth (the value actualized through contribution, creation, and achievement). Both are real and measurable.

### Worth Calculation Protocols

Detailed protocols for calculating worth across different domains and contexts. This includes algorithms for aggregating subjective assessments, weighting different types of contribution, and handling edge cases and disputes.

### Contribution Metrics

Specific metrics and measures for different types of contribution—creative work, care labor, ecological restoration, community building, knowledge creation, and more. Each domain has specialized metrics while remaining commensurable through UDS.

### Post-Scarcity Coordination

Worth economics enables coordination in conditions of abundance by focusing on contribution and flourishing rather than scarcity and competition. This section explores how the system handles resource allocation without traditional market mechanisms.

### Value Theory Foundations

The philosophical and theoretical foundations of Worth economics, including its relationship to traditional economic theories, labor theory of value, and contemporary post-capitalist thought.
